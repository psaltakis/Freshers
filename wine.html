From dealcoholised to five years in oak

The list for this evening spans very nearly the full stylistic range. A dealcoholised Blanc de Blancs, a tank-method Prosecco from the Treviso DOC, an off-dry Rheingau Riesling off a south-facing quartzite slope, Marlborough Sauvignon Blanc, a Côtes de Provence rosé, Pinot Noir from the basalt of the Auvergne, two structured French reds, a Californian Zinfandel at 15.2 per cent, and a fortified Samos Muscat with five years of oxidative élevage behind it.

None of this is a question of quality ranking. These wines are structurally different from one another, so the list is arranged by body rather than by price or critic score, and each entry sets out its acidity, tannin, alcohol and residual sugar so the comparison rests on something firmer than adjectives.

Body is read from structure rather than from the label: fruit concentration, tannin, élevage and residual sugar, with alcohol only one part of it. The Cloudy Bay is the most aromatic wine here and still only medium-bodied. The Samos carries a hollow marker because its weight is sugar and cask age rather than tannin, so it sits apart from the scale rather than at the end of it.
